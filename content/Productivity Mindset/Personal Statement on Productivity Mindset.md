# Personal Statement on Productivity Mindset
I've been struggling with burnout for a while. Since leaving the structure, security, and support systems of my life in the US, I've struggled with my ability to accomplish work as I would like to. "Productivity" is the name we usually use to describe one's ability to accomplish things, but this term is practically radioactive to me — loaded with baggage that is hostile to my core values and lived experiences. By deconstructing my issues with productivity and reframing them in a mindset that works for me, I can more effectively navigate my relationship to work.

## Unpacking my issues with productivity
**Anti-capitalism**
From a Marxist critique of productivity, the ideal productive person works like a machine. It needs no breaks or sick days, is not distracted, makes no errors, and has perfect consistency in its output. Its goals are in perfect alignment with its employer. In this view, the practices of productivity tame the messiness of the human worker toward the imagined perfect automaton.

**Labor and disability**
While no human can live up to this standard, disability exacerbates this gap. In "work/life balance" disabilities add difficulty to both sides, and the burden of organizing accommodations adds on top of that. In my lived experience, this means my capacity to work can be precarious, and easily unbalanced.

**Despairing era**
It's a moment of emerging climate catastrophe, rising authoritarianism, and increasing instability in the western imperial core. In the moment, it's hard to summon the importance of menial tasks. In orienting myself toward goals, there is instability beneath any plans for the future.

However, I think the purpose of acknowledging injustice and inhumanity should be to precipitate action. I want my response to a toxic productivity culture to be action toward a better world.

## Reframing productivity
**Time as opportunity**
In *Saving Time* (2023), Jenny Odell writes about many different ways of understanding time. One is her interpretation of the Ancient Greek *kairos*, where time is not a scarce resource to be spent, but a moment of unique possibility. This combats my despair over the future in an important way. Some mental models encourage optimism through ignorance, repression, or magical thinking. However, this idea of "seizing the time" claims the future as unwritten and begins at the question of how you can have an impact.

This means that as I pursue work, I can seek out opportunities for what I think is politically urgent. As I reconstruct my personal support systems and domestic life, I have the opportunity to explore new modes of living that reflect my anti-capitalist and disability justice values.

**Foundation of self-reflection**
Vital to unpacking and exploring these goals is internal clarity. Journaling has been my most useful tool in pursuing this clarity. Additionally, as I orient myself toward impact, a writing practice develop my ability to communicate novel ideas through writing.

I'm interested in a form of productivity based in "seizing the time," where I center myself on what action in this moment builds toward the future I want, using self reflection tools to clarify that future.
