# Situation
I shifted my third goal several times through the program. Initially Prioritization, I planned to experiment with different project management and organizational tools, document my process thoroughly, and implement them in group work. Ultimately, I shifted to a productivity mindset ([[PPD Goal - Productivity Mindset]]) to better reflect my needs as they emerged. This maintained elements of prioritization, like time management and organization practices, but shifted away from evaluating success on performance of the tactics. Instead, the aim was to develop self-knowledge. 
# Task
I read four books for this goal: [[Saving Time]], [[Your Head is a Houseboat]], [[Managing to Change the World]], and [[Getting Things Done]].

In terms of organization, I experimented with [[Project Management Systems]] including software Trello, pen and paper task tracking, and digital documents. I experimented with the software Obsidian over five iterations to organize my project management and [[Digital Gardening]]. 

In order to improve my time management and focus, I experimented with the [[Pomodoro]] technique, a personal [[Journaling]] practice, and implementing an [[RSS Feed]]. 

I synthesized these experiences into a [[Personal Statement on Productivity Mindset]] and a [[Personal Productivity Statement Addendum]]. I received feedback on the initial statement from my sibling, Kit, who is my go-to confidant and copyeditor. I wrote a [[Personal Productivity Statement Addendum]] 

The end goal of this project was to develop a healthier relationship to work that reflects my needs in this transitional period of my life.
# Action
In my first semester, I tried organizing my to-do lists in binders, notebooks, software like Trello, and digital documents in my word processor. This was part of my initial PPD goal of "Prioritization" before eventually revising it to Productivity Mindset.

Starting in the 2026, I focused on iterating my use of one software, Obsidian, for personal organization. In [[Obsidian V1]], I intended to use it as an all-purpose tool for organizing personal and work related information. In [[Obsidian V2]], I specifically used it as a project management system for my final project. In [[Obsidian V3]], I experimented with setting up a digital garden. In [[Obsidian V4 (This site)]], I implemented a complementary software, Quartz, to publish publicly online. In [[Obsidian V5]], I created a vault for project management by customizing the software to fit my PMS interface preferences.

Over the summer, I reflected on what I gained from my experimentation and reading about organization systems and time management. I found that the learning I gained was better synthesized as the development of a mindset, rather than a change in performance. I wrote an initial [[Personal Statement on Productivity Mindset]], which reconciled my personal politics and goals with my critiques of productivity culture. Kit provided feedback that I could better articulate the connection between my writing practice and desired impact. Then, I wrote [[Personal Productivity Statement Addendum]] to expand on that topic as well as the role software plays in my productivity.
# Result
Through the process, I found a more appropriate goal for my current needs and relationship with work. I believe I achieved my desired result of developing a healthier relationship to work for this new era of my life.

My productivity mindset itself is reflected in my personal statement and addendum.
Through reading and writing on productivity, I have developed a more positive mindset for my future, where my work is an investment in a better world. 

Through experimentation, I expanded my metaphorical toolbelt of time management techniques, complementing my cognitive tools with more practical daily practices.

Also in my practical toolbelt is the ability to customize open-source software to fit my needs. This has opened up a suite of values-aligned software tools that give me more agency over my organization workflows.
# Reflection
My progress on this goal was deeply intertwined with my personal difficulties during my studies. Ultimately, at this time, it was more achievable to develop more grounded knowledge of myself and practical tools regarding productivity, rather than finding ways to optimize my output. With these techniques and framing tools I have a more values-aligned, disability-friendly foundation to build on. 

In terms of next steps, my priority is to develop [[Obsidian V5]] as my project management setup. First, I will get the interface to a place I'm happy with, specifically adding color coding and further customizing the task properties and templates. I will also revisit [[Getting Things Done]], as a text on effective workflows, in this process. Then, I can flesh out the tasks to reflect the full scope of my goals and obligations.

A lower priority item is to expand on [[Obsidian V4 (This site)]] as a digital garden, bringing in my pre-existing resources I've documented, and adding new ones as they emerge.
