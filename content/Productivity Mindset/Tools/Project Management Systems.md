# About
The first semester ended up being a series of iterations on formats to organize task lists. Initially, I purchased a dedicated binder for PPD projects. I used it a couple times but realized I was never referencing it because it was too big and bulky. I tried simplifying it to a to do list notebook, but never got into the habit of looking at it. I figured a system I could reference on my phone would always be with me, so I tried Trello. But once I set it up, I never looked at it again. The fourth and final system I tried was a personal wiki in Obsidian. I was unable to build this into a prioritization habit, but the software has replaced most uses of Microsoft Word in my project management workflow. This was part of my initial PPD goal of "Prioritization" before eventually revising it to Productivity Mindset.

# Reflection
One reason these specific systems didn't work was a lack of stability in my workspace. After having a stable home office — customized to my ergonomic needs — for several years, I was adjusting to my new space, where I had limited control over my ergonomic environment. I was also adjusting back to school work from office work and from a long term team with shared organization systems to a short term team finding its own organization system.

I made a big miscalculation in this initial goal. With the constraint of the goal being measurable, I felt I needed monitor my prioritization via my work output. The pressure of being graded on my organization and productivity made it more stressful for me to implement organization systems. When issues with my mental and physical health made working more difficult, I got into a doom loop of stress. Monitoring my performance in this way impeded my learning. For this reason, I ultimately pivoted my goal to exploring options and developing a mindset, to remove the grade-related performance pressure from the essential task of project management.
