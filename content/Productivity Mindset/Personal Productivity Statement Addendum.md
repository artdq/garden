I wanted to follow up from my [[Personal Statement on Productivity Mindset]] on two topics:
1. How does writing orient me toward impact? *(Based on feedback from Kit)*
2. Software(a) plays a large role in modern productivity advice, as well as my practices for this learning goal. What role does does software play in productivity for me? 

## Writing & Impact
I think writing is one of the most important political tools, particularly in shaping ideas and sharing them persuasively. While the messaging isn't always transmitted through the written word, writing is often part of the process, often in developing salient messaging. I see it as a tool to understand oneself, to connect with others, and to build shared understanding. To communicate with others beyond the bounds of embedded assumptions (and therefore to collaborate in ways beyond the status quo), I rely on writing to build toward shared understanding. On the micro level, this can be articulating norms, values, and work styles between collaborators. On the macro level, this can be sharing a vision for political change. Writing helps facilitate my impact by enhancing my ability to communicate ideas. 

## Software
Values-wise, I'm deeply opposed to the American big tech landscape. While I believe in a future where platforms owned by Google and Meta are not required for mass communication, we are not there yet. In areas where I can, I'm moving away from software products that don't align with my values: ones that protect user privacy, practice transparency, and support user agency to use the software as they see fit — that do not punish you for modifying or leaving it. These are not merely issues of political affiliation, but of consumer rights and freedoms.

An idea that has become salient to my use of software has been the "centaur and reverse centaur" from Corey Doctorow, based in a politics of labor and power relations. (Paraphrased from https://pluralistic.net/2026/08/03/andor/#either; he uses this metaphor to talk about AI, but it's applicable across technology adoption)
> The first group of workers(...) are "centaurs": people assisted by machines; workers who choose the time and manner of (technology) adoption. The second group are "reverse centaurs": people recruited to serve as peripherals for machines, which direct their actions and workflow

The centaurs are often satisfied with their work and enthusiastic about technology, the reverse centaurs are often exploited and degraded. 

These are the frameworks through which I want to seek productivity through software: tools that I have the freedom to choose the time and manner in which I adopt them. 

Open source software has been an exciting avenue for this. Granted, there is a learning curve to any software, but I do not see this learning process as "time wasted." Rather, it's investing time into developing workflows from the workers' perspective. And at this tipping point in the technological and political world order, making informed, empowering choices about software use could be highly influential to the future.


*(a) While the term technology is broadly used to refer to computing software and hardware these days, I think it's important to maintain perspective on the wide world of technologies. Computers aren't the exclusive domain of "tech experts". Software isn't the only method of technological improvement. Codifying the divide between "wise software company" and "hapless consumer" only entrenches power.*
