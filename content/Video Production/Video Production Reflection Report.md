# Situation
My second goal was to professionalize my video production skillset toward internet-native applications. I planned to accomplish this through online courses, content analysis, and independent projects.
# Task
My end goal in studying video production is to lead video projects for myself and clients. As a creative, I am interested in collaborating with grassroots, countercultural, and independent voices. I want my production process to reflect professional standards, and the aesthetics to suit the intended media environment.

The available courses I could find were aimed at film or corporate video production, which are evidently different in format, production capacity, and aesthetic from internet native video. The main video production courses I completed were through LinkedIn Learning (See #course ). They were taught by documentary filmmaker Ashley Kennedy and followed a case study where she was the director, producer, and editor of the film. While not focused on the internet-native pipeline, the genre (documentary) and work style (one primary creator) most closely resembled the type of content I'm interested in.

I supplemented this with researching the filmic language of internet-native or video. Specifically looking at content creators I admire, I analyzed videos and watched interviews.  (See #video-analysis , #interview , #essay )

I practiced several segments of the production process through independent projects, including concepting, filming, organizing footage, and assembling an edit.
# Action
I worked on producing video for 5 projects:

[[Dutch Design Week]] - Pre-production and production 
In October, I developed a pre-production plan and completed filming for a travel vlog about  Dutch Design Week.

[[Medicijn Reel]] - Post-production
In the winter, I edited a short video for a client through my coach Kevin Brouwer, who gave me feedback. This feedback highlighted how I can better handle audio to remove distractions and add more energy.

[[Pitchbook Prototypes]] - Production
In the second semester, I participated in shooting video for pitchbook prototypes (a video podcast and reel). I didn’t have a pre-determined role showing up to film, so I fell into the role of production assistant, anticipating problems, troubleshooting technology, and making suggestions.

[[Keukenhof Reel]] - Production and Post-production 
In the spring, I shot footage for a short-form reel at the Keukenhof. Here, I practiced capturing b-roll, organizing footage, and assembling an edit. (Documentation: Appendix 10, Videos: Appendices 11 and 12)

[[Knitting Vlog]] - Pre-production and production
In August, I developed my concept for a knitting project vlog format to promote the sale of my forthcoming knitting patterns. I developed the overarching content goals, story arc, and messaging; identified and sourced the gear needed for production; and put together 3 video tests for the key types of shots. (Documentation: Appendix 13, Videos: Appendices 14-16)

Research
The most impactful source was the video essay “Why Youtubers Hold Microphones Now”, which explores the tension between professional and “Internet Ugly” signifiers in video production. For example, the choice of holding a lavalier mic holds in-group cultural significance but is not indiscriminately messy: notably it doesn’t sacrifice the audio quality. (Nicolas, 2024) This analysis influenced how I made production decisions for my knitting vlog.

# Result
Through this process, I completed projects in the 3 phases of video production.

I have furthered my knowledge around the documentary video production process, from pre-production planning to post-production organizing and editing. I have developed my understanding of the filmic language of long-form internet native video. I also better understand the on-set roles and types of media companies I am interested in pursuing. 
# Reflection
While not in through the exact projects I had intended, I did successfully achieve my intended learning outcomes. Some specifics of my initial plan needed to be adjusted. The initial production pace I had planned turned out to be unrealistic for my personal capacity. The planned project type—travel vlogs—also turned out to be poorly suited. However, I was still able to practice all parts of the production pipeline in the learning process. I also identified a genre niche more suited to my abilities and creative interests — knitting vlogs.

The LinkedIn Learning courses provided useful organizational tools and workflows to make the process faster and easier. While the course explained that a more detailed-pre-production plan makes for an easier post-production process, I felt this experience firsthand in my practice projects. This impacted how I am approaching pre-production on my knitting vlog. 

To further my video production skills, I am working toward completing the pilot of my knitting vlog. My next steps are to finish pre-production on this project: turn my broad video outline into a detailed script and production plan; gather graphic and audio assets. I am also continuing to build my video editing skills and knowledge of Premiere Pro through LinkedIn Learning.
