# About
Dan Olson of Folding Ideas is one of the content creators I admire most. He produces deeply researched video essays/documentaries with highly polished production quality, often thorough critiques of cultural figures and topics.

Video:
What It Really Takes to Grow as a Creator with Calgary's Dan Olson of Folding Ideas | AVOLA Extended
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_pnW2ppNk5c

# Reflection
While most of this wasn't super surprising, some of his framing leant credence to some ideas I've been intuitively following:
- Looking at production in a "hybrid" manner: through the lenses of both traditional filmmaking and YouTube 
- Balance thinking of your channel as a brand and as an artist with a vision
Amid discussions of media as marketing tools, it's so refreshing to hear professionals talk about media as an art form. 
# Notes
Sees more success short form grow to long form than vice versa (miniminuteman, stanzipotenza)

"Hybridize" production - leaning on both filmmaking and YouTube vernacular

Explaining complex ideas - informed by Pedagogy of the Oppressed
challenge - forgetting the intro level material the audience needs

How he tests ideas - 
Twitter/Bluesky - approach idea; do people respond; are there obvious flaws
Test audience - what doesn't make sense, what it all adds up to

Production roles
- creators should focus on fundamentals - writing, presentation
	- custom video, graphics help, but you can use a lot of found visuals 
	- production style can follow creative interests
	- Examples - hbomberguy works with a producer, other help is as needed; philosophytube has a broader team, and that helps her get out videos of a consistent quality on a consistent schedule; Shawn - just him; Noah Caldwell-Gervais shoots some (intros/outros) low budget and DIY and people like it -- people respond positively to the DIY, no reason to change it 
* Hiring an editor (or other role) - the deciding question: are there projects you *didn't* do because *you'd* have to edit them?

People starting out will always have to grapple with other people doing it better, longer, with more resources - came into his own around 2015

Conscious of how platform affordances form user interaction - blip TV is oriented toward series vs youtube sidebar oriented toward discovery, drop in chaotically

Balance thinking of your channel as a brand (synergy, IP) and as an artist who wants to see things in the world

His advice for creators:
- embrace the chaos: find energy through joy or anger, wherever you can
- Fundamentals - writing, have a good idea; performance, practice performance, record yourself and make yourself edit it, learn to tolerate the sound of  your own voice
- Remake someone else's video to figure out how it works
- Understand why you like stuff
