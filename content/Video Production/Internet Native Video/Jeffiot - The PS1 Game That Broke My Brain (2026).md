# About
As I was watching this video essay, I noticed how sophisticated the moment-to-moment editing was. I conducted a close reading of the essay with observations about style, cinematographic grammar, and essay structure, with attention to what I find characteristic of the medium.

Because internet video trends are fast moving and genres are idiosyncratic, the formal elements don't have as much language or literature as older media formats. Therefore, learning from observation is important. 
# Highlights
- As aesthetic choices, the disregard for the fourth wall can be seen like a disregard for "professionalism" in an Internet Ugly aesthetic sort-of way. It also builds a more relatable and authentic speaker persona. 
- Jumpcuts can enhance the flow of the writing, and with motion effects, cropping, blocking, the meaning can be drawn out in nuanced ways.
- There is so much reference humor couched in pastiche with an artfully effortless execution which feels very characteristic of meme culture.

Talking head Videography eg 4:08-4:30
- Same shot setup the whole time, but tons of cuts manage the pacing. Almost one per second emphasizing specific words, zooming in to point to props
	- Zoom transition with sound effect to kick off
	- Longer shots have a subtle zoom in or out effect, shorter shots are often static
	- Close ups of controllers are tracked to the slight sway of the prop
	- At 4:25, the video lingers for a couple frames while the audio jumps to the next line
		- Keeps his deadpan expression for another moment -- bringing out the joke in the line preceding ("the naked-looking smaller controller"), without feeling like it pauses for laughter
- Like a decade of YouTube cinematography backing this style: 
	- Vlogs and the hyperreal - how the jumpcut is used in vlogs to mediate pacing https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GSnktB2N2sQ
	- Jumpcuts are Here to stay - the teleporting jumpcut for "parenthetical statements" - asides, differentiating jokes https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XvK8xtVbopA
Analysis: Jumpcuts can enhance the flow of the writing, and with motion effects, cropping, blocking, the meaning can be drawn out in nuanced ways.


Joke tangent 23:56
- 23:56 - EA character - backing music, fade to black transitions, second camera angle, voice filter, with slowdown effect on key phrase
	- This is striking true at criticisms against EA in the community. Definitely signals in-group, but shows it's parody, not earnest speculation
- 24:31 - sort-of-character, half-hearted trump parody "crooked" "sleepy" namecalling
	- this specific joke is so overplayed, it would be cringe to put in the effort to do it earnestly (e.g. voice) - nonchalance conveys this understanding that it isn't a smart or original joke
	- flames/devil horns over graphics convey the jokiness -- the contrast is so over the top, you know it isn't earnest
- 38:00 - fancam effect 
- 38:05 - TV-style donation appeal pastiche
- 38:13 - meme format
Comedic editing
- This second angle/different framing to switch between modes is common on YouTube, but also on TV - e.g. asides in the intro during Taskmaster. Like breaking character, bringing the audience as a confidant
- The joke isn't sold through the performance as much as it is through editing. The delivery remains very conversational and casual -- doesn't feel overly rehearsed or try-hard
	- This feels so connected to the layers of irony and manufactured authenticity in internet culture -- an effortful, calculated combination of signals that feel effortless and authentic
- It's not just earnest illustration where the showing and telling have the same message; it shows self-awareness
	- The use of irony holds true to "The Good News of Like Share Subscribe" https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TQoM-QF52Zo
Analysis: There is so much reference humor couched in pastiche with an artfully effortless execution which feels very characteristic of meme culture

Graphics
- "camera" wiggles as it moves through fuller illustration
- flat assets rendered in 3d space make the visualization feel a bit more unexpected, less corporate
- Photocutouts as icon pop-up alongside talking heads -- makes it a bit more immediate, especially helps with names, locations

Interviews
- Jumpcuts within interviews to pump up pacing
- cutting in archival and illustrative footage
- subtitles for folks with heavier accents
- Key story moments told with clips from everyone -- also very documentary-style; shows consensus importance of event to insiders
