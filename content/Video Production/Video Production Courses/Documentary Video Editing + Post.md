# About
This is the third course in the documentary video series, focused on the post-production phase.
# Notes
Going into edit, review assets:
- Watch and listen to everything: organize and take notes
- Review checklist of what needs to be included from which speakers to hit the story beats
- Review interview highlights, which subjects may be leaned on more or less
- Outside editors should get production notes (from pre-production and production)
- In-software tools to organize: categorizing and tagging clips, placing markers, adding notes in metadata. An assistant editor would help with organization.
- Two column script from pre-production can now include real dialog etc

Main edit should get rounds of feedback before picture lock, when things like color correction, motion graphics are added

In the project asset bin, folders can categorize asset types (e.g. b roll, interviews, music, stock images, temp music, etc). Sub folders organize further: interviews organized by the full shot vs clips; b roll organized by activity or location. Folders for sequences in progress as well (e.g. one for WIP scenes, and one for rough cuts to assemble together).

Notetaking:
Identify keywords (relating to themes and subject) to label with metadata
Markers can include notes, can be expanded to full section
Make it a subclip to make it searchable

Building a narrative from the interviews:

Beginning assembly:
- Separate into thematic select sequences based on the pre-production plan
- Start with a mega cut of the theme
- Grab all relevant subclips
- Narrow it down, looking for flow and structure
- The goal isn't a final cut, it's narrowing down the material to make the *first* cut

Bringing sequences together into a master sequence:
- Select sequences can be brough into the master sequence as "nests" to keep them grouped up
- The "radio edit" just focuses on the basics of what you hear, not looking for visuals or timing finesse
- Finesse the beginning first to establish the language for the rest of the edit

Bringing in additional visuals
- cover up jump cuts
- direct associations - illustrate what's being discussed
- using photos - motion like a zoom in or out can reinforce emotions
- maintain focus on speaker for high impact
	- if you need to cover a jump cut, you can punch in or use a morph cut

After first rough cut, step back and look with fresh eyes. Consider big picture structure questions -- is the story clear?

Polishing enough for audience feedback:
- Can make adjustments to source material before adding to timeline
- Audio
	- speech should peak between -12 - -6
	- can use key frames if the variations in audio are too big
	- mixing speaker, bg music, and ambient channels  so they mesh together
- Color correction
	- First, individual shots
	- Then, check the whole scene matches
	- Finally, a specific look or  style (aka color grading)

Screening for feedback
- Types of audience: to target audience -- is it resonating with people close to the topic?; outside of TA -- is it too niche? are the more universal elements engaging?; other filmmakers -- craft feedback
- Process: sent the video link, talked through pre-prepared questions
- Capture all feedback together -- look for common themes

Save each version separately

Finishing steps: Color correction; Sound mixing; Motion graphics
