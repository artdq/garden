# About
This is the second course in the documentary video series, focused on the production phase.
# Notes
Planning documents:
- production itinerary with all details for video crew: agenda with times, locations, travel plans
- Client event agenda
Available to everyone in a google drive

Interviews
- interview schedule with timing, subject, location, and any key details
	- include time to setup and strike set, travel time etc
- Interview questions in document

B-roll and observational
- shot list: break down with camera techniques, notes on composition etc

Gear list
- equipment notes/manuals for major equip

Roles of director:
- visionary: a clear idea of the look and story (refined through the pre-production phase). Communicated with . Open to tweaking it with feedback
- Interviewer: Must be able to evaluate interview content, emotion, and subtext; assess responses. Shape the interview toward what the film needs or find new source.
- On set manager: own the production plan, maintain relationships, communicate. Make sure you get the shot. As producer - Ensure everyone is prepared with logistics.

Setting up interview: 
- location: on the interviewees turf can be more comfortable and communicate about the subject. Backgrounds can be all different or all consistent. Green screen can keep it consistent
- Shot details: 
	- Framing usually medium or close ups.
	- Two cameras can make editing smoother. High fidelity can make extreme close ups easy.
	- Positioning of subject: consider rule of thirds, weight between left and right
	- Staging: consider what is in the background
	- On axis (subject speaks to camera) vs off axis (subject speaks to interviewer)
	- Lighting: the key is tonally appropriateness
	- Focal length - aka is the background in focus, slightly out of focus, or very out of focus
- Who does the interview? Producer or director may be the right fit case-by-case. If rapport from the pre-interview helps vs if fresh perspective helps.
- Plan, set up framing, light it before subject arrives

Conducting interview:
- mirror tone and body language you're looking for
- Spiel before interview - etiquette explainer, big picture road map 
- gently steer
- Don't read questions, make it feel like a conversation
- keep open ended phrasing
- start more concrete easy, then get out
- maintain eye contact and give visual (not audible) feedback
- end with any final words, questions
- Get 20 sec room tone

Challenging interviews:
- if struggling to get comfortable:  interview them while they're doing something they love (relevant to the subject). Can be revealing in actions and get them relaxed 
- several interviewees: one person can dominate the conversation or it may make one anxious
- Sometimes crew etc can be helpful or harmful to the energy
- Difficult questions: devils advocate framing can help separate you from the question - "some critics may say X, what do you have to say"
- Laddering techniques can help

B-roll
- needs to cover jump cuts
- can illustrate subjects of speaking
- can add information, context, emotional/thematic weight to dialog
- can orient setting, including time
- Get variation in wide, medium, and close shots
- Consider movement, especially in establishing shots
- Work with the setting, natural focal points
- Shooting subjects: try to be a fly on the wall. A zoom lens helps you get closer shots from a distance. Doing it after the interview can inform the types of shots you want
- Capture ambient audio, even when it'll be over interviews. Can be flat without it.

Observational scenes
- don't want to obstruct natural proceedings, ask someone to repeat something
- Multi-cam helps get coverage (wide, close-up)
	- Note the 180 degree rule
- shoot cutaways from the same moment
- For esp a repetitive task - continuously reframing a shot can give you coverage to edit into a sequence

Gear
- E.g. one main cam, a smaller backup, and an action cam. Lenses
- Accessories like tripods
- Field monitor
- LEDs, battery power
- Lav and shotgun mics
- laptops and SD cards, back up everything at the end of every day

Lighting: 
- key light 
- fill light
- back light
- background light if needed

Sound:
capture redundant - lav + shotgun on a boom

Non-shine make-up
color chart (for color correction)
publicity release

For each shooting observational, consider what shots you want for the story, what gear is appropriate for the setting
Handheld vs tripod
Lenses needed for different types of shots
When you know you'll want quality audio

While shooting, slating everything is preferable. But at least note key moments for the edit and update shot list.

Media management:
- Move to hard drives, might use software like ShotPut Pro
- Bring into Premiere
- rename every file: shoot day, location, description
- save as raw and transcoded files for editor
