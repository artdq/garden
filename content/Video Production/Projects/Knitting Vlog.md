# About
In my final video project, I developed my concept for a knitting project vlog format to promote the sale of my forthcoming knitting patterns. The format takes the popular project vlog genre but instead documents my design process (rather than the pattern purchaser's process of knitting the garment). It focuses on the pre-production phase.
# Concepting
Mindmapping the emotional experience and desired features of executing a knitting pattern. My conclusions here shape my design criteria for what makes a good knitting pattern and what messaging I want in the vlogs.

# Knitting Vlog Plan
What: A 15-minute project vlog documenting the design of the knitting pattern

## Strategy
Video goal: Drive awareness of featured pattern. 
	Key metrics: video views, ravelry views/faves/purchases (where the pattern is sold), 
Channel goal: Build brand awareness as creator; build an audience.
	Key metrics: overall watchtime, views, followers

Intended effect on the audience: help them imagine making the project. Hit the key beats emotionally and as checkpoints in the process. Make the experience of knitting the pattern feel desirable, achievable, and satisfying (see mindmapping) through how the process and final garment is depicted. Additionally, the release of the video as part of the broader pattern rollout should make the pattern release feel like an exciting event.

## Creative direction
About the broader designer brand: playful, menswear-adjacent knitting patterns. 

Where mainstream fashion puts people in a box (via gender, self-expression, bodytype), handmade clothing is a way to go against the grain. The garments are expressive and cleverly constructed. They fit into a "menswear" wardrobe and are designed for that style of fit, but embrace color, pattern, and shape in ways often limited to womenswear. Patterns are written for an inclusive audience re: accessibility, gendered language, and sizing. Designs often play with and subvert themes of traditional masculinity. The audience isn't strictly men. Menswear is treated as a genre of fashion, rather than a description of the wearer.

Brand archetype: the wacky inventor — I develop my own techniques and garment constructions through experimentation.

Note about the patterns as products - the USP is the artistic point of view. Behind-the-scenes information, inspiration, process etc reinforces the value.

Relevant cultural trends: slow fashion; anti-consumption; body diversity; positive masculinity

## Inspiration Channels

- Westknits - tutorials and pattern announcements with strong "you can do it" messaging. Always includes bright, graphic pattern samples
- Ash Bentley - each video is a vlog of a contained project. Very candid, with a focus on the creative process. Sense of humor, authenticity, creativity throughout style — documenting mistakes, bad habits, as well as wins. Basically no forth wall, transparent about thought process, pivoting projects. The final project reveal at the end is excellent catharsis — the spectacle and commitment to the bit is very memorable.
- Bea creative knits - a knitting vlogger, very laid back style and pacing. Also includes pattern design in vlog, but is not the central story. Lots of lifestyle elements.
- Answer in Progress - a video essay channel that documents their exploration and learning about a topic through a project. E.g. a project investigating "why do my glasses keep slipping down?" includes a historic overview of glasses design, fabricating a DIY solution on the journey to the answer. Somewhere between cultural studies and journalism. Great for how they present information and do storytelling of a project process.
Broadly, project vlogs often have a story arc of posing a question and working toward the answer, and barriers build tension.

## Story Outline
The story arc of each video will follow the design process from concept to finished sample. Key beats of knitting a garment are featured.

Act I: Vision
Introduce the primary question — this is my concept, can I pull it off? Outline the concept of the garment, how the idea originated. Visuals feature development swatches, a sketch of the schematic.
* Process beats: talking through color selection and choosing yarn -- the kickoff of any knitting project. Make it approachable -- many people worry about choosing the right yarn.
- Video: primarily talking to camera, semi-scripted, with supplementary graphics.

Act II: Process
The trials and tribulations of knitting the project
- Process beats: Make it imaginable by hitting the highs and lows of knitting a garment, needing to unravel and redo, etc. Make it more approachable through technique tutorials.
- Video: Conversational, spontaneous speaking to the camera structure the narrative. Key techniques are highlighted via tutorials in top-down close ups. Montages of observational b-roll.

Act III: The reveal
The catharsis and answer to the initial question. It documents the product photoshoot for the pattern release. Don't show the finished garment until the reveal.
- Process beats: The excitement of the finished object, the reflection on whether it turned out like you hoped.
- Video: B-roll of photo shoot edited in the style of a sizzle reel -- slo-mo, fashion editorial type shots. Intercut with talking-to-camera wrap-up, reflecting on how the project turned out.

Annotations:
- A (uncropped and cropped) - not sure why I even tried a time lapse -- the purpose of shooting knitting at this angle is instruction on the detailed hand movements. Obvious issues with the framing -- I didn't yet have a tripod or way to secure my phone for the angle.
- B - The framing was able to be slightly improved, but still need gear to get the frame. Lighting issues with the white, semi-reflective table.
- C - Tripod helped get some height. Placement of the camera + standing at the table made it hard to hold my hands consistently in frame. C1 - see autofocus issues. C2 - resting my hands on the table makes the framing and focus more consistent.
- D - I was able to DIY a bit more height with the tripod for a better top-down angle. There are some unpleasant shadows on the top and left sides. However, with the camera across from me, the angle is slightly less POV, and it's hard to monitor the framing.
- E - light bounces placed just out of frame make the lighting more even. Placing the camera between me and my hands helps me monitor the framing while filming. E1 - the wider lens shows a bit more, but the small screen makes it hard to make the precise hand movements needed while monitoring. It may also be harder for the audience to follow. E2 - the closer lens has a more appropriate cropping.
There seem to be compression issues (?) with the video export, making the footage look blown out. Needs troubleshooting. Otherwise, I'm satisfied with this filming setup. 

The next step for these shots is planning and performing an actual tutorial.
### Conversational style (in zip folder)
<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/91LXvPz63WA?si=gSoRy3jXPDnwF-NR" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>
Prep for this was mostly unscripted. I like the off-the-cuff style, but I want to prepare talking points for the next version. Especially since this will be the opening, I need to hone the talking points more closely, and be more focused in what I want to communicate about the design and intentional about what techniques I want to explain. I could more clearly articulate the design goal ("posing the question" of the video), and emphasize the inventiveness of the design.

The audio is a little quiet. There are distracting breaths that are annoying to cut around. This might be mitigated in my performance and/or how the audio is captured.

The framing and natural lighting worked well. I like the contrast between speaking further away and getting in close to create intimacy when letting the viewer in on the details. This staging language can be explored a bit more. 

Editing-process-wise -- there is probably a faster workflow to cut down the ums, likes, and awkward pauses. I'll also need to find a good workflow for handling the audio transitions between clips. 

In the next version, I also want to plan intentional stage directions and what key moments I might want to punctuate specific editing techniques (zooming in, changing up the rhythm, etc).
