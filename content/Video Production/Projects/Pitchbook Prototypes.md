# About
For my team's pitchbook assignment, we worked on two video-oriented prototypes, a video podcast and an IG reel. We set one production day for both prototypes. I assisted on the shoot day, with no predetermined role. Other teammates were leading the process on each prototype, but planning was limited. Going in, we had scripts and rented gear but had not set further roles or a timetable.

For the podcast recording, I stepped in to operate the audio equipment, setting it up, connecting it to my device, finding recording software, troubleshooting issues, and monitoring/co-directing the performers. I also prepared a short description of the assets and context to send with the recordings our audio editor. I also stepped in to record some voice over.

For the reel recording, I stepped in to operate the camera and helped the director flesh out the script to specific shots.

Across the day, I did some production-assistant type tasks, too, identifying and attending to issues as they arose — coordinating personnel on the spot, fixing details like stray coffee cups in the shot.

When we needed to re-record the podcast audio, I was more proactive on set, performing as one of the main characters, and directing the other performer.
# Reflection
While the production was a bit disorganized, it gave me the chance to consider what roles I may be interested in working toward. 

Production assistant: The shoot day really drew on my experience in event production -- working flexibly and with attention to detail to keep things running smoothly. Admin and organization isn't my favorite, but I can do it as needed.

Recording audio: I drew on past experience working with sound systems and USB mics. I felt good in troubleshooting a simple audio setup, but I don't know much about choosing equipment or evaluating audio quality beyond "good enough". However, I don't have much foundational aesthetic knowledge of audio, so this is an area I generally can level-up in. I don't see myself specializing in audio, but I'd like to be more competent here.

Recording video: I feel much more comfortable in visual than audio media. My eye is more trained for film analysis than camera operation, but I have a baseline of aesthetic literacy here. I'm still intimidated by professional grade equipment and the technical details of lenses, focus, and lighting. 

Performing: While recording voice-overs, I don’t normally think of myself as a performer, but I do enjoy pre-scripted vocal performance. I don’t particularly like the spotlight, but it’s a skill I would be willing to develop in the right context. 

Directing: This experience gave me some confidence in potentially directing. I've always been a bit intimidated by producing narrative video, since I don't feel comfortable writing in that space. However, I could imagine it more after helping adapt the script to a shotlist, and shaping the vision on set. I was surprised at how comfortable I felt directing performers, giving instructions and feedback while mitigating awkwardness.

Working toward independent content creation, I want to build fundamentals in all of these roles. In terms of what excites me most, directing and recording video were my favorite. I didn't hate any of the other roles, but they are a means to an end for me.
